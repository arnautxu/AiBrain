# Pattern catalog

Use this catalog to find candidates, not offences. Preserve a choice when it carries meaning, matches the author's sample, or suits the format.

## 1. Unsupported meaning

### Fabricated specificity

The rewrite adds a number, customer, anecdote, workflow, date, quote, feature, or result that the source never established.

Fix: remove it, label it as hypothetical, or ask for evidence.

### Vague authority

Examples: "experts agree", "industry reports suggest", "many believe", "research shows" without a named source.

Fix: name the supplied source or state the supported claim without borrowed authority.

### Promise laundering

Vague intent becomes a commitment such as "we'll investigate", "we'll follow up", "secure", "auditable", or "guaranteed".

Fix: preserve the original promise boundary and uncertainty.

## 2. Empty framing

### Throat-clearing

Examples: "here's the thing", "it's worth noting", "let's dive in", "in today's world", "the key point is".

Fix: delete the frame and begin with the point.

### Question restatement

The answer repeats what the reader asked before answering.

Fix: answer directly unless a short clarification prevents misunderstanding.

### Faux-insight setup

Examples: "what nobody tells you", "the part everyone misses", "the uncomfortable truth".

Fix: remove the claim to special insight and make the underlying point earn attention.

### Importance labels

Examples: "this matters", "the implications are significant", "a pivotal moment", "a crucial role".

Fix: show the consequence or cut the label.

## 3. Inflated or portable language

### Promotional fog

Clusters of words such as seamless, robust, transformative, cutting-edge, vibrant, empower, leverage, elevate, unlock, foster, tapestry, landscape, realm, pivotal, and testament often replace mechanisms.

Fix: name what changes, who acts, and what the reader can observe. Do not synonym-swap one vague word for another.

### Portability

A sentence could move unchanged to another person, company, product, or industry.

Fix: replace it with a supported mechanism, example, constraint, consequence, or judgment specific to the subject.

### Abstract noun pile

Examples: "optimization of operational efficiency", "facilitation of alignment".

Fix: make a person or system perform a concrete verb.

### Copula avoidance and false agency

Examples: "serves as", "stands as", "the decision emerged", "the data tells us".

Fix: use "is" or "has" when clearer, and name the actor responsible for the action.

## 4. Formulaic structure

### Binary reveal

Examples: "It's not X. It's Y", "not only X but Y", or repeated "not X, but Y" constructions.

Fix: state Y directly. Keep a contrast only when both sides genuinely carry the argument.

### Negative list

Example: "Not a tool. Not a framework. A movement."

Fix: state the positive claim.

### Colon reveal

Example: "The secret: a second agent grades it."

Fix: write a normal sentence unless the colon introduces a real list, label, quotation, or explanation.

### Padded triplet

A list has three items because the cadence expects three, not because reality does.

Fix: keep the true number of items. Do not ban genuine groups of three.

### Trailing analysis

An `-ing` tail claims significance without explaining causality: highlighting, underscoring, showcasing, reflecting, fostering.

Fix: state the actual relationship or stop after the fact.

### Repeated paragraph template

Every section uses topic sentence, explanation, example, and summary in the same proportions.

Fix: let the material determine section length and shape.

## 5. Mechanical cadence

### Stacked fragments

Several short declarations imitate drama: "No friction. No waiting. Just results."

Fix: connect the ideas. Preserve a single earned fragment when it matches the voice.

### Metronomic sentences

Several consecutive sentences have similar length and syntax.

Fix: vary length because ideas need different space, not to satisfy a numerical pattern.

### Repeated openers

Paragraphs or sentences begin with the same transition, demonstrative, or subject.

Fix: merge related ideas or change the logical connection.

### Synonym cycling

The same thing receives a new name in every sentence.

Fix: repeat the clearest term.

### Demonstrative kicker

Examples: "That changes everything", "This is where the risk hides", "That's the point".

Fix: cut it or fold a real consequence into the preceding sentence.

## 6. Formatting and punctuation tells

### Decorative formatting

Examples: emoji headings, bold words scattered for emphasis, tiny sections under large headings, or a table where two sentences work better.

Fix: use structure only when it improves scanning or comparison.

### Punctuation tic

Em dashes, semicolons, ellipses, colons, or exclamation marks repeat as a signature regardless of context.

Fix: vary or simplify them. A punctuation mark alone proves nothing.

### Markdown leakage

Headings, bold markers, or bullets appear in email, SMS, or another plain-text destination.

Fix: match the native format.

## 7. Weak endings

### Recap ending

The last paragraph repeats the piece with "overall", "in conclusion", or "ultimately".

Fix: end on the last concrete implication, decision, image, or next action.

### Generic uplift

Examples: "the future looks bright", "exciting times lie ahead", "the possibilities are endless".

Fix: cut it or use a supported next step.

### Manufactured mic drop

A clever metaphor or aphorism replaces the conclusion.

Fix: delete it if the previous concrete sentence already lands better.

## False positives to protect

Do not automatically remove:

- formal vocabulary used precisely;
- a deliberate triplet or contrast;
- one short emphatic sentence;
- passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant;
- an em dash characteristic of the writer;
- mixed registers, self-corrections, odd details, unresolved tension, slang, or profanity;
- quotations and proper names containing watched phrases.

Look for clusters and ask whether the writer could defend the choice. Over-editing strong human prose is a failure.
