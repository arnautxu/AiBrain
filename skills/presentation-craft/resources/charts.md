# Editable evidence and numerical review

Use native editable charts and tables. Retain requested rows, periods, categories, units and meaningful precision. Calculate totals once and reuse the same values in the chart, title and notes. Independently check sums, ratios, percentages, growth rates and displayed rounding. Use localized number formatting consistently. Source data, chart data and narrative must agree.

Choose the graphic for the question: trend, comparison, composition or reconciliation. Reduce gridlines and repeated labels; highlight the actual finding and annotate it near the data. Avoid giant donut charts when sorted bars communicate the comparison better. Do not add causes unsupported by evidence.

For a waterfall, the opening total and closing total start at zero; intermediate changes float from the running balance. Preserve signs and verify opening + changes = closing. The final EBITDA is a total, not another floating increment. For percentages, verify the authoring API's expected scale rather than assuming 31 and 0.31 are interchangeable. Keep numbers and percent/currency symbols together without accidental line wrapping.

Chart labels must identify the correct series and remain readable. Hide invisible offset-series labels and outlines. Preserve editable chart data, and do not replace source workbook formulas or links with invented ones. Reopen/check package data using available local tools when required; report unavailable target-application testing honestly. A PDF render cannot prove editability in PowerPoint or Google Slides.
